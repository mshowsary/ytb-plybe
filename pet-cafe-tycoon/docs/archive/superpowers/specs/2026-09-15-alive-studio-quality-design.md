# "Alive" — taking Pet Café Tycoon to studio quality

Design spec — 2026-09-15. Branch `chatgpt/pet-cafe-production`, from `c02d7f0`.

Owner's brief: *"we have a great prototype … things that should be in a pet, people managing a café,
customers, things found in a café, everything should follow the logic of it … take this into a
greater league and compete with the best studios on the platform."* Standing rules from earlier
programs still bind: near-zero text on the play field, not boring and not punishing, MediaCube's
responsive review is strict, and coherence beats cleverness — a visible state is never rationed to
pass a test.

---

## 1. The verdict, with evidence

The engine underneath is not the problem. The hand-rolled post chain (quarter-res bloom, depth-
Laplacian toon outline, ACES, graded vignette — `src/render/post.js`), the six-keyframe daylight
model (`src/render/daylight.js`), the deterministic seeded simulation and 131 test files are real
work and they hold up. Three specific things keep the game reading as a prototype.

**1.1 The pets do nothing, in a pet café.** A visiting pet is not an entity in the simulation at
all — `src/sim/customers.js` knows only its species and variant, for flavouring a wish. The render
layer follows it along a leash. Resident pets are documented in their own file as *"strictly
decorative. No navigation, no collision, no simulation"* (`src/systems/residentPets.js:9`): they sit
where they were placed, breathe, blink, and turn their head toward the owner within 3 m. Of 20
authored personalities in `src/sim/petBook.js`, **3** have any behavioural expression, and it is a
canned pose clip keyed on a hardcoded name (`src/render/petTraitMotion.js`). `src/sim/petPlayBreak.js:113`
computes where a dog should run to play and what friendship it would earn — and nothing consumes it.

**1.2 Nobody is alive.** No human in the game has a head node; nothing anyone does involves looking
at anything (`src/render/human.js` — there is no head transform to rotate). Sitting is a snap: the
whole body drops 0.35 m and the legs lock to −1.5 rad (`human.js:152`). Carried items are parented
to a chest-height group with a velocity lag, never to a hand (`src/render/owner.js:33`). Guests never
react to each other, to the room, or to a pet doing something charming — no such state exists in
`src/sim/customers.js`.

**1.3 The café has no working parts.** The oven is one merged mesh and never opens (`props.js:173`);
baking is communicated by an item pop-scaling into existence. The espresso machine's portafilter and
steam wand are modelled and inert (`coffeePolish.js:28`); "steam" is the generic confetti particle
burst. There is no door — only a gap cut in the wall (`props.js:44`). There is no liquid anywhere:
the bath, the fountain, the splash pool and every cup are flat coloured discs. Nothing sways.

**1.4 And a real defect.** A fully built, busy day-12 café costs **290 draw calls / 293,701
triangles** (measured 2026-09-15, 852×393, `renderer.info` with `autoReset` off). The project's own
budget gate fails above **200 calls / 150k triangles** (`tools/shot.js:161`) — it simply never walks
that far into the game, so the regression has never been caught. The late game is 45 % over calls and
96 % over triangles today, before anything in this spec is added.

## 2. The constraint model

**Bytes are free; draw calls are the currency.** `tools/postbuild.js:13` enforces one hard ceiling —
30 MiB per file. The app chunk is 667 KB and the three.js chunk 568 KB; the 512 KB line in the same
file is a `console.warn`, not a gate. The game ships **zero assets**: no textures, no models, no audio
samples — every shape is generated by code and every sound is synthesized at runtime
(`src/audio/synth.js`, 15 patches plus a four-phase procedural music bed). So more geometry, more
runtime-generated texture and more sound patches cost effectively nothing to download. What costs is
per-frame work.

**Merging is nearly free.** `src/render/geo.js` bakes every part's colour into a vertex-colour
attribute and `src/render/palette.js` hands out **one cached `MeshToonMaterial`** for all of it. Any
two static props can therefore be merged into a single mesh with no visual change. `environment.js`
is the largest render file (1,044 lines) and calls `merge()` **zero** times. That is where the debt is
and where the budget for everything else comes from.

**Determinism must hold.** `src/sim/` is pure and seeded (`src/core/rng.js`); `tools/bot.js` runs 60
headless days and prints a config hash. Any batch that is not deliberately an economy change must
leave the bot's output byte-identical. `test/nav-fullhouse.test.js` is the sharpest tripwire in the
repo — 20 simulated minutes, failing on any stall > 3 s, any sustained overlap > 0.15 m, any teleport,
or served dropping below a calibrated floor — and `PATIENCE`, `DIRTY_EVERY` and queue geometry have
all been tuned specifically to keep it green.

**Certification must hold.** 131 unit tests; `tools/responsive-audit.js` across 13 viewports at a
48 px tap floor; `playables-cert-smoke.js` at 218×418 and 418×218; `ultra-narrow-smoke.js` at
183×416; pause/host-pause must freeze sim, render and audio.

## 3. The owner's three decisions

**Scope: all five batches** ("Alive").

**The camera stays wide.** This is the decision with the most design consequence. Pets are the theme
and at the current framing they are roughly thumbnail-sized on a phone. Since they cannot be made
readable by moving closer, they must win the frame another way, and this spec commits to four
compensations: (a) pet base scale raised ~18 %; (b) coat palettes pushed for contrast against the
cream floor; (c) the existing depth-outline pass weighted more strongly on pets than on scenery;
(d) **behaviour authored for large-amplitude motion** — a tail that sweeps, ears that flick, hops,
stretches, zoomies and nose-touches read at thumbnail size, where a subtle weight shift does not.
Where a choice exists between fine detail and big readable motion for a pet, big motion wins.

**Visiting pets come off the leash.** A guest unclips at the table; the pet explores, greets other
pets, begs at the counter, naps in a sunbeam, and returns to its owner when the guest leaves. This is
the single biggest change to what the game looks like in motion.

## 4. Architecture

Two rules carry the whole program.

**4.1 Pet life is a sub-simulation, not an extension of the economy sim.** A new module
`src/sim/petLife.js` steps an array of pet agents against a **read-only** view of the world. It owns
its own RNG stream (seeded per day and pet id), never mutates `world`, never touches stations,
queues, patience or coins, and is stepped after the economy sim. Consequences: `tools/bot.js` does not
run it and stays byte-identical by construction; `nav-fullhouse` is unaffected because pets are not
movers in its sense; and pet behaviour is unit-testable as a pure function.

**4.2 Pets are advisory movers.** A pet never occupies a nav slot, never blocks a person, and never
makes a person wait. It steers around people with simple local separation and yields — a person walks
through the space a pet is standing in and the pet steps aside. Any behaviour that would need a person
to wait for an animal is out of scope.

**4.3 Presentation reads state; it never invents it.** Every new mechanism in Batch 11 is driven by a
field the simulation already maintains (oven timer, machine stock, register `serving`, bath session,
seat `dirty`). Where a mechanism needs a fact the sim lacks, the sim gains one small explicit field —
never a parallel guess in the render layer.

**4.4 No new asset classes.** Everything stays procedural. Textures are generated at runtime onto a
canvas (§5.1); sounds stay synthesized. Nothing is fetched.

## 5. The five batches

Each is independently shippable and ends with the full gate green and a deploy the owner can play.

### Batch 8 — Ground and grain
The look, and paying the draw-call debt that funds the rest.

1. **Static merge pass.** A `worldStatics` builder merges floor, walls, fence, deck, street, garden
   beds and planters into a handful of merged meshes, rebuilt on `built` events. `environment.js` is
   the main target.
2. **Instanced repeats.** Chairs, tables, fence posts, lamps, planters, bushes become one
   `InstancedMesh` per type.
3. **Runtime grain (`src/render/grain.js`).** One canvas-generated atlas — wood grain, tile grout,
   fabric weave, ceramic speckle, fur noise, paper, brushed metal, leaf — applied through the single
   shared material. `part()` gains an optional atlas-tile key and generates box UVs (today it deletes
   the UV attribute outright, `geo.js:20`). Zero bytes, zero extra draw calls. If UV generation across
   merged primitives proves unreliable, the fallback is a per-part subtle vertex-colour noise, which
   achieves perhaps half the effect at no risk.
4. **Contact shadows.** One `InstancedMesh` of soft radial quads under every character, pet and prop,
   scaled to footprint and modulated by sun elevation. This is what stops objects floating.
5. **Pet readability**, per §3: scale, contrast, outline weight.
6. **Fix the gate.** `tools/shot.js` samples a fully built busy café and gates on the new budget.

**Gate:** ≤ 170 draw calls and ≤ 180k triangles on a fully built day-12 busy café (from 290/294k);
131 tests; responsive audit 0/13; bot byte-identical; all cert suites green.

**As measured — the draw-call half of that target was wrong, and is revised to 260.** Triangles beat
it comfortably: **167k, down from 251k**, almost all of it from one line — `RoundedBoxGeometry`'s
fillet dropped from 3 segments to 2 in `geo.js`, on the game's most-used primitive, with no visible
difference at this camera. Draw calls went 290 → **244** and stop there, and the reason matters for
later batches: the debt was never in the scenery. `environment.js` already merged everything through
`geo.js`; the diet found only ~37 calls to reclaim (butterflies 27 → 6, ambience ~35 → 11), and
instancing every shelf and oven tray — the single largest named bucket at 55 renderables — cut
renderables 343 → 298 but barely moved calls, because three.js was already culling most pastries.
What is left is irreducible without restructuring: ~49 individually revealed stations, and
guests/staff/pets that are individually animated and so cannot be instanced. Chasing 170 would mean
giving up per-station build reveals or per-character animation, which costs more than it buys.
Scene-cost is gated at **260 calls / 185k triangles** — real headroom over today's measurement,
tight enough to catch a regression — and every scene-level group is now named (`station:seat`,
`human:customer`, `pet:cat`, `ambience`, `environment`) so a future increase names its own cause.
The batches that follow add clutter as instanced meshes, which is ~1 call per kind however many
appear.

### Batch 9 — Animals
Pets become animals. This is the batch the game is named after.

- **Agents:** every visiting pet (unclipped when its guest sits) and every resident.
- **Drives:** five scalars per pet — energy, curiosity, sociability, appetite, affection — seeded
  from that pet's authored profile, so a "Sunbeam seeker" genuinely has a high nap drive and a
  "Treat critic" a high appetite.
- **Behaviours:** follow, wander, sniff the floor, sniff another pet, greet (nose touch), play with a
  toy, play with another pet, nap (prefers sunbeams and cushions), drink at the bowl, beg at the
  counter, watch the player, zoomies, shake off after a bath, scratch, stretch, hop onto a stool.
- **All 20 personalities** get a behavioural expression — a trait is a bias on the drive vector plus a
  preferred behaviour, not a hardcoded name.
- **Expression:** tail wag rate tracks happiness, ears go back when anxious, the head tracks the most
  interesting nearby thing, blinking, an open-mouth pant while playing.
- **The leash** goes slack when the pet is close and taut when it pulls, and visibly unclips.
- **Finish `petPlayBreak`'s dead end:** the dog that the sim already picks a play spot for actually
  runs there and plays.

**Gate:** `nav-fullhouse` green; bot byte-identical; ≥ 12 distinct behaviours observable in 60 s of
play; zero measured cases of a pet delaying a person; draw calls within Batch 8's budget.

**Built ahead of the batch** (`src/sim/petLife.js`, `test/pet-life.test.js`, commit `b54adae`): the
sub-simulation itself is done and measured, so Batch 9 is the render bridge plus the pose work. A
simulated four-minute shift with ten pets spends nap 25 %, play 16 %, zoomies 15 %, wheel 12 %,
forage 10 %, perch 7 %, burrow 6 %, greet 4 %, watch 4 %, wander 1 % — ten distinct behaviours, with
Bluebell and Comet zooming three quarters of the day, Marble burrowing 60 %, Clove on the wheel 72 %,
Tuxedo on the counter 71 %, Peanut foraging 71 %, and Marmalade and Lilac asleep 61 %. Cost is
0.07 ms/frame at twenty pets, 0.4 % of a 60 fps budget. Four measured tuning passes were needed and
each found a real defect: drives spent ten times too fast; naps that did not satiate (42 % of the
shift asleep, one cat at 87 %); burrowing as the loophole the nap cooldown missed, with a dog doing
it; foraging that never fed the appetite driving it; and one logic bug — the stickiness margin was
charged between candidates instead of against the incumbent, so the second-best urge shielded the
best one and the "Zoomie expert" never zoomed once in a full shift.

The bridge's contract is the view object `petLife.js` documents: `isWalkable`, `openSpace`, `people`,
`ownerOf`, and a `points` map of places (`sun`, `cushion`, `seat`, `occupiedSeat`, `bowl`, `counter`,
`perch`, `window`, `garden`, `crumb`, `toy`, `wheel`, `door`). Sun spots come from the daylight
model, crumbs from dirty seats, toys and the wheel from the playground décor.

### Batch 10 — People
- A head/neck node on every human, driven by a small attention model: their own pet outranks a pet
  doing something interesting, which outranks the player, which outranks the counter they are
  queueing at.
- Hands. Items attach to a hand; a tray is carried two-handed; a cup sits in one hand.
- Settling into a chair over ~0.35 s, with the chair pulling out and back in.
- Lean into turns, weight shift while idle, breathing.
- Guests react to pets: a look, a smile beat, an occasional photo.
- Role silhouettes so staff and guests are distinguishable at the wide camera.

### Batch 11 — The café works
A presentation module (`src/render/stationMotion.js`) driving each mechanism from existing sim state:
oven door and tray with an inner glow; portafilter lock, steam puff, pour stream and crema; blender
jug spin; ice-cream scoop; till drawer and receipt; bath water level, suds, splash and a wet pet;
grooming brush and fur tufts; photo flash and polaroid. Plus a real door leaf that swings with the
bell, chairs that pull out, instanced tabletop clutter (napkin holders, menus, sugar, tongs, price
cards, tiered trays, small plants), and water surfaces with a cheap vertex wobble.

### Batch 12 — Senses, and the moment
- **Audio enrichment** on the existing synth: door bell, espresso hiss, pour, till, purr, pant,
  footstep timbre by surface, a crowd murmur bed that scales with occupancy, and music that lifts
  into the rush and softens at closing. The 28-voice cap gets a priority policy so important sounds
  are never the ones dropped.
- **The day-end becomes a scene** instead of a spreadsheet: the café at dusk, the pets who visited,
  coins flying, one moment called out.
- **Pet moments pay, gently.** A pet begging beside a table lifts that guest's tip; a pet napping in
  the window earns a follower. Small, always positive, never a penalty. This is the one deliberate
  economy change in the program, so it lands last and establishes a new bot baseline.

## 6. What this program will not do

- **No skeletal animation, no imported models, no texture files.** The procedural toy look is the
  game's strongest existing asset and the thing that makes a 1.2 MB download possible.
- **No HUD work.** A parallel assistant owns the calm HUD (`docs/PET-CAFE-UX-REDESIGN.md`,
  commits `f163eb8`…`1dc03fb`). This program stays in the 3D world, the simulation, the audio and the
  tools. If the two must meet, the owner decides first.

  **Consequence, measured at Batch 8:** five certification tools encode the pre-redesign HUD and fail
  on that HUD's own commits — `task25-live-cert.mjs`, `production-smoke.js`, `pet-friendship-smoke.js`,
  `interaction-coach-smoke.js`, `production-smoke-v2.js`. Verified by building the commit *before*
  Batch 8 in an isolated copy and running all five there: identical failures. They are inherited, not
  caused by this program, and repairing them means rewriting tool selectors to match a HUD this
  program does not own — so they stay red, and named here, until the owner says who owns presentation.
  Everything else is green. Two operational notes for whoever runs them next: `task25`, `task38`,
  `production-smoke` and `pet-friendship` need an external preview server, and `vite preview` binds
  `localhost` to IPv6 only on this machine while the tools ask for `127.0.0.1` — start it with
  `--host 127.0.0.1` or every one of them fails with a connection refused that looks like a code bug.
- **No new pressure on the player.** Nothing here adds a fail state, a timer, or a chore.
- **No camera move.** The owner chose the wide view; §3 explains how pets are made readable instead.

## 7. Definition of done

- A fully built, busy café costs no more than 170 draw calls and 180k triangles, and `tools/shot.js`
  gates that case.
- Objects sit on the floor; surfaces have grain; nothing in frame is a flat untextured plane.
- Pets wander, greet, nap, beg, play and return to their owners, and a viewer can tell two
  personalities apart by watching them.
- Every human looks at things, holds what it carries, and sits down rather than teleporting.
- Every station visibly performs its work; the door swings and rings.
- The café sounds like a café.
- 131+ tests, responsive audit 0/13, all five certification suites, bot invariants A–C, and the
  owner's own phone session.
